# E-PL9: first Gold 200 / Portra 400 color drafts

## Outcome, 2026-11-04

The owner has now exported **two actual full-resolution RAW developments** with independently specified CMIO coefficient sets using OM Workspace. The first Gold 200-inspired draft is slightly warmer in the supplied scene; the Portra 400-inspired draft is more restrained in chroma. Both have distinct pixels. These are **v0 color-only proposals**, not calibrated film emulations, a Classic Chrome implementation, output from modified camera firmware, or an installed ART replacement.

**Follow-up:** [neutral balance and tonal trials v1/v2](epl9-tonal-neutral-trials.md) now establish an effective stock contrast/H/S/M path in actual Workspace exports. The attempted v1 CMIO gamma attachment had no effect in its isolated pixel control. This page retains the original v0 experiment and its limits.

This follows a successful [CMIO control experiment](epl9-workspace-cmio-bridge.md): copied ORFs differing only in captured coefficients produced a clear, directionally correct saturation change in the software renderer. The exact numeric proposals are versioned in [the draft definitions](../recipes/epl9-film-drafts-v0.json). Images, ORFs, tables, configurations and private metadata remain ignored under `local/`.

## Proposal and native coefficient correspondence

Each recipe defines twelve physical `(H,S,L)` records, in signed native coefficient units. The tool checks the two accepted identities exactly:

- `gold200-draft-v0`: future Watercolor I identity `0x01010078`.
- `portra400-draft-v0`: future Watercolor II identity `0x01020078`.

Those identities are manifest bookkeeping for the accepted replacements. The desktop files retain the original Natural metadata and use the two qualified CMIO banks. **No Watercolor-to-COLOR executable hook is installed**, and ordinary camera modes are not rewritten. Original diagnostic markers in the historical job probes remain unchanged.

Gold's initial design explores warm-sector color and limits blue/green intensity. Portra's initial design reduces chroma more and raises selected color-sector lightness. Exact sector/hue direction labels remain an inference from shared-table research, not a full E-PL9 wheel calibration. Since these are chromatic-sector changes, they do not provide an independent neutral white-balance shift or a luminance toe/shoulder curve. WB, exposure, matrix, RGB gamma, luminance tone, detail, noise reduction and grain are deliberately held at their source values during this first isolated trial.

The owner's previously chosen digital reference direction is retained: Ritchie Roesch's [Gold 200 recipe](https://fujixweekly.com/2020/04/16/my-fujifilm-x-t30-kodak-gold-200-film-simulation-recipe/) and [Portra 400 v2 recipe](https://fujixweekly.com/2020/12/30/fujifilm-x100v-film-simulation-recipe-kodak-portra-400-v2/), checked 2026-11-04. Their Fujifilm values and Classic Chrome base are not copied into Olympus coefficients. The values in this project are original engineering proposals, not fitted matches to those photographs or measured Kodak film responses. No third-party photographs are downloaded or redistributed for this trial.

The optional `--native-config` check executes the E-PL9 full-table packer at **`0x6079e50e`**, with no software pixel implementation or device-register replay. Each case executes **1133 native instructions**, returns with balanced stack and passes the independent **whole 5200-byte configuration** oracle. All 36 coefficient values in the resulting twelve 24-byte records exactly match the same recipe values inserted into CMIO P. The complete 3028-byte native source table and 5200-byte output configuration are saved locally. This checks coefficient transport/layout, not equality of the hardware and software pixel algorithms.

## Observed TIFF pixels

| Export | Pixels different from neutral | Mean absolute RGB difference (/255) | Maximum | Mean channel range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| CMIO COLOR neutral | baseline | 0 | 0 | 32.9016 |
| Gold draft v0 | **74.5853%** | **0.5224** | 7 | 32.7091 |
| Portra draft v0 | **90.1254%** | **1.4230** | 12 | 29.6635 |

Gold and Portra differ from one another in **86.3400%** of pixels, with mean absolute RGB difference **1.2746/255**, maximum 17. A high fraction of changed pixels does not mean a strong look: Gold's changes are particularly subtle in this frame. Its mean R rises and B falls versus neutral, consistent with the slight warmth observed in this particular scene. Portra's mean channel range falls by approximately **9.84%**. This is an RGB range proxy, not a colorimetric saturation measurement or a claim of film fidelity.

None of these three TIFFs has a channel at 255. The source scene therefore cannot establish highlight roll-off performance. A few channels reach zero (fractions recorded privately); no conclusion about a film-style black toe follows from their count. Visual inspection shows retained detail, but no edge sharpness metric, exact geometric equivalence, isolated H/L response or repeat-render tolerance has been qualified here.

## Remaining work

The next consequential controls are neutral color balance and a qualified tone-curve route, followed by additional daylight/neutral/highlight scenes and owner review. An initial static trace now identifies E-PL9 CMIO top-level serialization at `0x607689b3`, primary header/dispatch at `0x60768a61`, and fixed T transfer at `0x60768a2f`. The latter points to the exact table payload already matched in the ORF. These are static observations; most serializer subfunctions and arbitrary tone attachment remain unqualified.

An increasing 256-halfword run near CMIO P `0x185c` does not by itself identify the luminance curve. Its range differs from the previously identified native tone curves; it has not been edited. No unknown array is relabeled as a tone control from monotonicity alone. The first drafts remain unchanged while this connection is investigated.

Final camera work still needs the native hook, caller/crop/CFA qualification, parameter ownership/cache invalidation, actual ISP completion/output, preview/capture/save behavior and unrelated-mode regressions. These successful desktop color trials do not remove the native hardware boundary in [the real-RAW report](epl9-real-raw-boundary.md).
